# Indoor and multi-floor shoots

Read when the request names an interior, asks for indoor coverage, or covers a venue with several relevant floors.

## Establish what can be mapped

Find official visitor maps and floor labels first. Photos can verify appearance, but isolated room photos do not prove room adjacency. Separate current public rooms from private event spaces, staff areas, closed rooms, and historic plans that may no longer describe visitor access. Use the venue's actual floor naming; do not assume “first floor” and “ground floor” mean the same thing.

For a venue such as Casa Loma, discover the floors and rooms from current sources rather than copying an assumed list from memory. Render each relevant verified floor that offers shooting spots. Do not arbitrarily cover only the ground floor, and do not fabricate an upper floor to satisfy a multi-map request. Show a floor with no usable portrait locations only when it is necessary to explain a route or the user requested comprehensive coverage.

If geometry is unavailable, try other credible plans and user-provided material. When named rooms are verified but adjacency is not, offer a clearly labeled “Schematic shooting locations — room connections unverified” sheet with independent spot groups and no invented route. State what is missing. Ask for a floor plan when a navigable room layout is essential; this can be done while completing independently verified floors or outdoor coverage. A schematic is not a verified floor plan.

## Floor composition and circulation

- Retain the reference's watercolor/ink character in an overhead cutaway: softly rendered floors and furnishings, legible wall lines and door openings, warm paper margins, orange stop markers. Keep corridors and room boundaries clearer than decorative textures.
- Use verified room proportions and connections as the geometric guide. Do not move walls or insert doors for aesthetics. Avoid roof coverings or perspective that hides rooms.
- Label each sheet with venue and floor. Keep the same palette, lettering, marker scale, and commentary treatment across sheets.
- Use stable spot numbers across the full session. Label the actual stair/lift transfer and destination sheet; route arrows must connect through real openings. Mark uncertain transfers as unverified rather than implying access.
- Draw a compass only if the plan's orientation is known. Otherwise retain the source-plan orientation and omit cardinal-direction claims.
- Keep furniture subordinate to circulation. Illustrative furnishings cannot establish that a prop or seat is available for use.

## Lighting and lenses indoors

Distinguish window-lit rooms, deep interiors, mixed artificial/daylight, and circulation spaces. Recommend a specific setup for the scene rather than “use natural light” everywhere.

- Window-lit portrait: place subjects beside or facing a verified window, choose framing for the available distance, and suggest fill only if allowed. Describe directional light conditionally when the window orientation or shooting time is unknown.
- Tight room: consider a 24–35mm environmental view with faces away from frame edges; do not default to an 85–135mm lens without enough distance.
- Larger room: consider 50–85mm for portraits when working space supports it, and wider framing for architecture. Protect group depth with an appropriate aperture.
- Low or mixed light: discuss a practical shutter speed for subject movement, workable ISO, and white-balance consistency. If permitted, suggest restrained bounced or softened flash; do not assume dark wood or colored ceilings make a neutral bounce surface.
- No-flash/no-tripod venue: propose handheld available-light settings and a suitable lens; do not suggest circumventing the policy. Avoid touching or moving heritage objects without permission.

In client-facing mode, keep this technical reasoning out of the deliverable. Show the intended room/spot and planned photo only.

## Multi-map checks

Confirm all promised floors have saved PNGs; names agree with sources; stop IDs are unique and match notes; transfers land on the right floor; restricted rooms are not presented as accessible stops; and client versions contain no technical photography notes. Missing evidence must not be concealed by a plausible-looking floor illustration.
